# About the Vulcean Continuity

The Vulcean Continuity is a worldbuilding project I started in early 2025. This project takes place on a fictional Earth-like planet (Arventia) in a fictional star system (Athovon System), which is located 21.9 light-years away from Earth in the constellation of Serpens Caput. This is also my first ever worldbuilding project, meaning that I may not be the best at this kind of thing 😅 (The main reason I started this project was because I had a story which I had created back in 2022 or so, and as of early 2025, I got into astronomy, and then when I found out about worldbuilding, I had decided to create the Vulcean Continuity)

### Namesake

The Vulcean Continuity is named after the hypothetical planet Vulcan that was thought to exist between the Sun and Mercury (which actually exists canonically in my lore, as well as Planet X), which has been disproven (in real life) by general relativity.

# About Habitable Worlds

Habitable Worlds is a public website catalogue which aims to let the public (Arventian inhabitants) know about potential candidate Arventia 2.0 analogues that have been discovered. Habitable Worlds was also developed by AIRA (Arventian Interplanetary Research Authority, which is the NASA analogue of my worldbuilding project) and Exoplanet Corporation, which is a sub-company led by a group of AIRA members.

# Lore (Vulcean Continuity)

The Vulcean Continuity takes place in the year 3000 (Earth years) and near the end of the year 3307 (Arventian years). This difference in years happens because an Arventian year is about 9.31% shorter than an Earth year.

Earth’s technology has advanced significantly over many centuries, especially in exoplanet science. Yet just like Tau Ceti, it still struggles to detect, let alone confirm, any planets within the Athovon System.

The Athovon System, the home stellar system of the main civilisation in this story (Arventians, who live on the Earth-like planet known as Arventia), sits 21.9 light-years from the Sun in the constellation of Serpens Caput. The system’s central star is a spectral type G7 main-sequence yellow dwarf star called Athovon. This system includes 12 major planets, ten dwarf planets, and many asteroids and comets.

Human exoplanet scientists have found evidence for two planets: One of which is 2.6 times the mass of Jupiter, orbiting at a distance slightly farther out than Jupiter’s orbit around the Sun, which was found with radial velocity data, and the other is a roughly Saturn-massed hot-Jupiter on a 7.6-day orbit, but no detection has been confirmed or disproven. As of 3000, the Athovon System remains one of many “planetless” stellar systems that continue to frustrate human exoplanet scientists, with planets “confirmed” only to be disputed later.

Arventians have known about humans and the Solar System for a long time. The fact that humans still haven’t confirmed a single planet in the Athovon System keeps Arventians calm. They don’t have to worry about a potential human invasion of their home planet for resources and colonisation.
