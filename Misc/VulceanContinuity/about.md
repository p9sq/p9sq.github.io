# About the Vulcean Continuity

The Vulcean Continuity is a world-building project I started in early 2025. This project takes place on a fictional planet (Arventia) in a fictional star system (Athovon System), which is located 21.9 light-years away from Earth in the constellation of Serpens Caput. This is also my first ever world building project, meaning that I may not be the best at this kind of thing 😅 (The main reason I started this project was because I had a story which I had created back in 2022 or so, and as of early 2025, I got into astronomy, and then when I found out about world building, I had decided to create the Vulcean Continuity)

### Namesake

The Vulcean Continuity is named after the hypothetical planet Vulcan that was thought to exist between the Sun and Mercury (which actually exists canonically in my lore, as well as Planet X), which has been disproven (in real life) by general relativity.

# About Habitable Worlds

Habitable Worlds is a public website catalogue which aims to let the public (Arventian inhabitants) know about potential candidate Arventia 2.0 analogues that have been discovered. Habitable Worlds was also developed by AIRA (Arventian Interplanetary Research Authority, which is the NASA analogue of my world-building project) and Exoplanet Corporation, which is a sub-company led by a group of AIRA members.

# Lore (Vulcean Continuity)

The Vulcean Continuity takes place in the year 3000 (Arventian years) and almost halfway through the year 2542 (Earth years). This difference in years happens because an Arventian year is about 15.25% shorter than an Earth year.

Earth’s technology has advanced significantly over many centuries, especially in exoplanet science. Yet just like Tau Ceti, it still struggles to detect, let alone confirm, any planets within the Athovon System.

The Athovon System, the home stellar system of the main civilisation in this story, sits 21.9 light-years from the Sun in the constellation of Serpens Caput. The system consists of a spectral type G7 main-sequence yellow dwarf star called Athovon and a spectral type K6 main-sequence orange dwarf star called Vexulan, plus ten planets, nine dwarf planets, and many asteroids and comets. The two stars orbit each other every 10.86 Earth days on an almost circular orbit with an eccentricity of 0.013. Both are tidally locked to one another. All planets, dwarf planets, comets, and asteroids orbit the system’s barycenter located between Athovon and Vexulan in a P-type orbital configuration.

On Earth, humans catalogue the Athovon System as a detached eclipsing binary, tracking only the stars, not the planets. This means the stars aren’t transferring mass and pass in front of each other from Earth’s perspective. Athovon and Vexulan orbit close together and rotate fast, once every 10.86 Earth days. Compare that to the Sun, which rotates once every 27 Earth days. This fast rotation drives strong magnetic activity in both stars, producing large sunspots on each other’s surfaces. Sunspots form where a star's surface is about 1000 °C cooler than its surroundings, making them look darker, and they form through interactions with the star’s magnetic field. On the Sun, these sunspots stay small. On Athovon and Vexulan, the stronger magnetic activity from their fast rotation would make them much bigger.

These large sunspots create major brightness fluctuations as the stars rotate, and combined with the slightly elliptical orbit, they distort the light curves human scientists rely on to spot a transiting planet. A planet passing in front of either star produces only a tiny dip in brightness, and spot activity buries that signal in noise. The system’s uneven Doppler shifts create a separate problem for the radial velocity method, making it hard to track a clean back-and-forth stellar wobble. Between the two effects, human exoplanet scientists have found evidence for a planet three times the mass of Jupiter orbiting at a similar distance to Jupiter’s orbit around the Sun, spotted through radial velocity data, but no detection has been confirmed or disproven. As of 2542, the Athovon System remains one of many “planetless” stellar systems that continue to frustrate human exoplanet scientists, with planets “confirmed” only to be disputed later.

The intelligent civilisation living on Arventia, the Athovon System’s habitable planet within Athovon’s habitable zone, calls themselves Arventians. They have known about humans and the Solar System for a long time. The fact that humans still haven’t confirmed a single planet in the Athovon System keeps Arventians calm. They don’t have to worry about a potential human invasion of their home planet for resources and colonisation.
