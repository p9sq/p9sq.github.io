Mercury | ☿

Overview

Mercury is the first planet from the Sun and the smallest in the Solar System. It is a rocky planet with a trace atmosphere and surface gravity slightly higher than that of Mars. Its surface resembles the Moon, with heavy cratering, long rilles, and bright ray systems formed by ejecta.

Orbit

Mercury has the most eccentric orbit of any planet in the Solar System, ranging from about 46 million to 70 million km from the Sun. It takes 87.969 Earth days to complete one orbit. Mercury's sidereal year and sidereal day sit in a 3:2 resonance, so one solar day, sunrise to sunrise, lasts around 176 Earth days.

Physical characteristics

Mercury's surface shows extensive cratering and mare-like plains, showing it has been geologically inactive for billions of years. Its largest crater, Caloris Planitia, spans 1,550 km, about a third of the planet's diameter. The impact that formed it triggered lava eruptions and left a mountainous ring about 2 km tall around the crater.

Surface conditions & Exosphere

Surface temperatures on Mercury range from 100 to 700 K, or about -173°C to 427°C. At the poles, temperatures never rise above 180 K due to the lack of atmosphere and a steep gradient between the equator and the poles. Despite the heat elsewhere, permanently shadowed polar craters stay cold enough to hold water ice. Mercury is too small and hot to hold onto a real atmosphere, so it has only a tenuous exosphere of hydrogen, helium, oxygen, sodium, calcium, and other elements, constantly lost to space and replenished from the solar wind and impacts.

Internal structure

Mercury's core takes up about 57% of its volume, compared to 17% for Earth. This makes Mercury's core proportionally the largest of any planet. Scientists think an early collision with a large planetesimal may have stripped away much of Mercury's original crust and mantle, leaving the iron-rich core behind as a bigger share of the planet.

Magnetic field & Magnetosphere

Despite its small size and slow rotation, Mercury has a significant global magnetic field, about 1.1% the strength of Earth's. This field is likely generated by a dynamo effect from the circulation of its iron-rich liquid core. The field is strong enough to deflect the solar wind and form a small magnetosphere around the planet.

Exploration

Mercury has been visited by three spacecraft. Mariner 10 completed the first flyby in 1974, mapping less than half the planet's surface. MESSENGER orbited Mercury from 2011 to 2015, mapping the whole planet and confirming water ice at the poles. BepiColombo, a joint ESA and JAXA mission, is set to enter orbit around Mercury in 2026.
