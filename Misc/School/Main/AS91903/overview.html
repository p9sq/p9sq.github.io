Overview of the Solar System

The Solar System has one star (the Sun), eight planets, and a huge number of smaller bodies.

The Sun

The Sun accounts for 99.8% of the Solar System's total mass, with Jupiter making up the remaining 0.2%. The Sun runs by fusing hydrogen into helium in its core. This process releases heat and light for everything in the Solar System. However, the temperature and amount of light received from the Sun decrease the further out you go.

The inner planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars are the closest planets to the Sun. These are rocky planets with solid surfaces.

Mercury is small and cratered, with no substantial atmosphere.

Venus has an atmosphere 93 times thicker than Earth's, which consists of Carbon Dioxide that traps heat, making it the hottest planet.

Earth is the only planet confirmed to host life.

Mars has a thin atmosphere and a reddish surface coloured by iron oxide.

The outer planets

Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune orbit farther from the Sun than Mars. These are the gas and ice giants.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System. It has a giant spot called "The Great Red Spot", which has been there for centuries.

Saturn is known for its prominent ring system, which is made of ice and rock particles.

Uranus is the only planet which rotates on its side, most likely from an ancient collision with the planet.

Neptune is the furthest planet in the Solar System and has the fastest winds in the Solar System, reaching over 2,000 km/h.

Smaller bodies

The asteroid belt orbits between Mars and Jupiter. It has millions of rocky asteroids, which are most likely remnants from the early Solar System. The largest members of the Asteroid Belt are Ceres, Vesta, Pallas, and Hygeia.

Beyond Neptune is the Kuiper Belt, which consists of many smaller icy bodies and dwarf planets, such as Pluto and Eris. Like the Asteroid Belt, it is likely a remnant of the early Solar System.

Comets come from the Kuiper Belt and the hypothetical Oort Cloud. As they come close to the Sun on their oval-shaped orbits, the increased heat causes the ice to melt off of their surfaces, which produces those famous comet tails.

Moons

Just like how Earth has a moon, many of the other planets have moons of their own. Jupiter has over 90 confirmed moons, including Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto, which are Jupiter's four largest moons. Saturn has over 200 confirmed moons, including Titan, which has an atmosphere thicker than Earth's and lakes of liquid methane.

Scale

Distances in the Solar System are huge. Light from the Sun takes about eight minutes to reach Earth, and it takes light over five hours to reach Neptune. Astronomers use the astronomical unit (AU), the distance between the Sun and Earth, to measure these distances easily. For example, Earth is exactly 1 AU away from the Sun on average, and Neptune is about 30.1 AU away from the Sun. This means that Neptune is about 30.1 times farther away from the Sun than Earth is.
