The town

A place unlike anywhere else

Greymouth sits at the mouth of the Grey River on the rugged West Coast — New Zealand's wildest, wettest, and most untamed region. It's the kind of place that doesn't try to impress you; it just does.

The West Coast has long been defined by coal, gold, and greenstone. That heritage is woven into the streets, the pubs, and the people. Whether you're passing through on the TranzAlpine or using Greymouth as a proper base, you'll find it more rewarding than you expected.

Get outside

The outdoors on your doorstep

The West Coast isn't a place you observe from behind glass. It's a place you put on your boots for.

🏔️
Paparoa National Park
Thirty minutes north. The Punakaiki Pancake Rocks and blowholes are unmissable, but the real reward is the limestone karst country — walking tracks through moss-draped forest that feels prehistoric.
🌊
Cobden Beach & Blaketown
The surf beach right on Greymouth's edge. Dramatic surf, driftwood-strewn sands, and the kind of sky that makes you want to stay well past sunset. Perfect for a long, mindless walk.
🚴
West Coast Wilderness Trail
One of New Zealand's Great Rides — four days through wetlands, rainforest, and gold-rush country. Sections available as day rides. Bike hire in town.
💎
Pounamu — Greenstone
The West Coast is the spiritual home of pounamu. Visit Shantytown Heritage Park or take a guided river trip to understand why this stone has been treasured for centuries.
🐦
Lake Brunner
A half-hour inland. Kayak the still waters in the morning, fish in the afternoon, or sit and watch the Kārearea falcons work the treeline above.
🚂
TranzAlpine Day Trip
The train journey through Arthur's Pass and the gorges is extraordinary. Watch the landscape change dramatically and come back knowing why New Zealand doesn't mess about with scenery.
© Stewart Nimmo
The Breakwater

Where the river meets the sea

The Grey River pushes into the Tasman Sea at Greymouth's northern edge. The breakwater is one of those places you walk out onto and immediately understand the West Coast — the scale, the wildness, the indifference of the elements to your presence.

On a stormy day, the waves crashing the rocks here are something to witness. Bring a coat.

After dark

Evenings worth staying up for

Greymouth isn't a nightclub town — and that's precisely why the evenings are so good. Cold beer, local food, and the kind of conversation you don't get anywhere else.

🍺
Monteith's Brewing Co.
Greymouth is the birthplace of Monteith's. The original West Coast brewery runs excellent tours where you can taste the full range straight from source. Book ahead — it's popular for a reason.
🥩
Local Kai
West Coast whitebait is legendary — eaten in a simple fritter with nothing but egg and butter. Find it in season (August–November) and consider it mandatory.
🎸
Live Music & Locals
The Coast Hotel and a handful of other spots host live music on weekends. West Coasters play for themselves; not performing for tourists, which is exactly what makes it worth showing up for.
🌌
Stargazing
Minimal light pollution means genuinely dark skies. On a clear night, drive five minutes out of town, pull over, and look up. The Milky Way overhead is worth the whole trip.
🎬
Regent Theatre
A beautifully restored Art Deco cinema right in the heart of town. There's something charming about watching a film in a small-town picture house on a rainy West Coast evening.
📸
Nimmo Photography Gallery
At 102 Mackay Street, Stewart Nimmo's gallery is a genuine highlight — award-winning fine art prints of the West Coast. Stewart is often on site, and he enjoys a good chat.
© Stewart Nimmo
After midnight

The Aurora overhead

Low light pollution means that when the Aurora Australis puts on a show, Greymouth sees it in full. Keep an eye on the forecasts and be ready to step outside.

Plan your visit

Getting here & getting around

Greymouth is well-connected for a town of its size — a natural stop on any South Island road trip, or a destination in its own right.

By Train TranzAlpine — Christchurch to Greymouth daily One of the world's great train journeys. 4.5 hours through the Alps.
By Road SH6 along the coast / SH73 via Arthur's Pass Both are scenic routes. SH6 from Hokitika is especially beautiful.
