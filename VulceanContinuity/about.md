# About the Vulcean Continuity

The Vulcean Continuity is a world-building project I started in early 2025. This project takes place on a fictional planet (Terrania) in a fictional star system (Phebe System. I know it's spelt "Phoebe" but in my story its spelt without the "o"), which is located 21.9 light-years away from Earth in the constellation of Serpens Caput. This is also my first ever world building project, meaning that I may not be the best at this kind of thing 😅 (The main reason I started this project was because I had a story which I had created back in 2022 or so, and as of early 2025, I got into astronomy, and then when I found out about world building, I had decided to create the Vulcean Continuity)

### Namesake

The Vulcean Continuity is named after the hypothetical planet Vulcan that was thought to exist between the Sun and Mercury (which actually exists canonically in my lore, as well as Planet X), which has been disproven (in real life) by general relativity.

# About Habitable Worlds

Habitable Worlds is a public website catalogue which aims to let the public (Terranian inhabitants) know about potential candidate Terrania 2.0 analogues that have been discovered. Habitable Worlds was also developed by TIRA (Terranian Interplanetary Research Authority, which is the NASA analogue of my world-building project) and Exoplanet Corporation, which is a sub-company led by a group of TIRA members.

# Lore (Vulcean Continuity)

The Vulcean Continuity takes place in the year 3000 (Terranian years) and the year 2445 (Earth years). This difference in years is because a Terranian year is longer than an Earth year.

Earth’s technology has advanced significantly over many centuries, especially in exoplanet science, but just like Tau Ceti, it still struggles to detect, let alone confirm, any planets within the Phebe System…

The Phebe System, which is the home stellar system of the main civilisation of this story, is a binary star system located 21.9 light-years away from the Sun in the constellation of Serpens Caput. The system consists of a spectral type G7 main sequence yellow dwarf star called Phebe and a spectral type K6 main sequence orange dwarf star called Amaros, seven planets, dwarf planets, and many asteroids and comets. The two orbit each other every 10.86 Earth days on an almost circular orbit with an eccentricity of 0.013. They are also both tidally locked to one another. All planets, dwarf planets, comets, and asteroids orbit the system’s barycenter located between Phebe and Amaros in a P-type orbital configuration.

On Earth, the Phebe System is known by humans (only the stars and not the planets) and is catalogued as a detached eclipsing binary. This means that the stars aren’t doing any mass transfer and “eclipse” each other from Earth’s perspective. Because Phebe and Amaros orbit so close and are rotating quite fast (10.86 Earth days. For comparison, the Sun rotates once every 27 Earth days), this causes them to produce large sunspots on each other. Sunspots are areas of a star where the surface is cooler by ~1000 C (causing them to appear darker than the surrounding surface) and are caused by interactions via the star’s magnetic field. For the Sun, these sunspots are small, but for Phebe and Amaros, these would be much bigger.

Because Phebe and Amaros produce sunspots on each other, and have a slightly elliptical orbit, this creates large uneven Doppler shifts that seem to “cover up” any planetary signals via the transit method. However, human exoplanet scientists have found evidence for a Jupiter-massed planet orbiting at a similar distance Jupiter orbits the Sun via radial velocity as they watch the system move backwards and forwards from their perspective, but no detection has been definitively confirmed or disproven. As of 2445, the Phebe System remains as one amongst many “planetless” stellar systems that continue to frustrate human exoplanet scientists, always having planets “confirmed” and then later disputed.

The intelligent civilisation that lives on Terrania, the Phebe System’s habitable planet located within Phebe’s habitable zone, call themselves Terranians. They have known about the existence of humans and the Solar System for quite some time. The fact that humans still haven’t confirmed a single planet in the Phebe System is what keeps Terranians calm, without having to have to worry about a potential human invasion of their home planet for resources and colonisation.
