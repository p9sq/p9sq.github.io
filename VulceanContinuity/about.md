# About the Vulcean Continuity

The Vulcean Continuity is a world-building project I started in early 2025. This project takes place on a fictional planet (Terrania) in a fictional star system (Phebe System. I know it's spelt "Phoebe" but in my story its spelt without the "o"), which is located 21.9 light-years away from Earth in the constellation of Serpens Caput. This is also my first ever world building project, meaning that I may not be the best at this kind of thing 😅 (The main reason I started this project was because I had a story which I had created back in 2022 or so, and as of early 2025, I got into astronomy, and then when I found out about world building, I had decided to create the Vulcean Continuity)

### Namesake

The Vulcean Continuity is named after the hypothetical planet Vulcan that was thought to exist between the Sun and Mercury (which actually exists canonically in my lore, as well as Planet X), which has been disproven (in real life) by general relativity.

# About Habitable Worlds

Habitable Worlds is a public website catalogue which aims to let the public (Terranian inhabitants) know about potential candidate Terrania 2.0 analogues that have been discovered. Habitable Worlds was also developed by TIRA (Terranian Interplanetary Research Authority, which is the NASA analogue of my world-building project) and Exoplanet Corporation, which is a sub-company led by a group of TIRA members.

# Lore (Vulcean Continuity)

The Vulcean Continuity occurs in 3000 (Terranian years) and 2445 (Earth years). Earth technology has gotten better (particularly in exoplanet science), but Earth cannot find the planets of the Phebe System, not yet, at least...

The Phebe System (where the main civilisation of this story is centred) is 21.9 light-years from the Sun,  in the constellation Serpens Caput. It consists of two stars,  a G7-class yellow dwarf called Phebe,  and a K6-class orange dwarf called Amaros. Both orbit their common centre of gravity once every 10.86 days and are extremely close together.  Every one of their planets, asteroids and comets orbits both stars simultaneously in a P-type system.

From Earth, Phebe and Amaros are observed as a detached eclipsing binary system that produces ellipsoidal variation, a system that does not easily fall into any of the available variable star categories. Both stars remain comfortably within each other’s Roche Lobe, and no mass transfer takes place, so the combined system’s complex behaviour is a result of geometry and tidal deformation, not some unusual stellar evolutionary path. The result of these different sources of variability creating complex reflecting effects gives one of the most complex home solar neighbourhood light curves that you would find, making a human astronomer’s job near impossible to differentiate between a real planetary transit signal and the system’s own flickering.  The radial velocity searches have received quite the same treatment, as the two stars produce regular, large asymmetric Doppler shifts that hide the planetary evidence. The tidally induced stellar activity from the starspots adds further complexity, and, given time, an occasional and notoriously contrary evaluation of a potential jovian planet several AU from the binary system centre has been collected from radial velocity data residuals, but no mark can survive peer review. As of 2445, Phebe is the most unmappable of the closest stellar systems out there, frustrating the latest exoplanetary science pushes.

The intelligent creatures native to Terrania, the life forms belonging to the Phebe System’s habitable planet, call themselves Terranians. They are aware of human existence and have been watching the Solar System for some time. Not having deciphered the photometric complexity of their nearest world is what keeps the Terranians unconcerned about a possible human invasion of their worlds, due to their increasing needs for resources and colonisation.
