# About the Vulcean Continuity

The Vulcean Continuity is a world-building project I started in early 2025. This project takes place on a fictional planet (Terrania) in a fictional star system (Phebe System. I know it's spelt "Phoebe" but in my story its spelt without the "o"), which is located 21.9 light-years away from Earth in the constellation of Serpens Caput. This is also my first ever world building project, meaning that I may not be the best at this kind of thing 😅 (The main reason I started this project was because I had a story which I had created back in 2022 or so, and as of early 2025, I got into astronomy, and then when I found out about world building, I had decided to create the Vulcean Continuity)

### Namesake

The Vulcean Continuity is named after the hypothetical planet Vulcan that was thought to exist between the Sun and Mercury (which actually exists canonically in my lore, as well as Planet X), which has been disproven (in real life) by general relativity.

# About Habitable Worlds

Habitable Worlds is a public website catalogue which aims to let the public (Terranian inhabitants) know about potential candidate Terrania 2.0 analogues that have been discovered. Habitable Worlds was also developed by TIRA (Terranian Interplanetary Research Authority, which is the NASA analogue of my world-building project) and Exoplanet Corporation, which is a sub-company led by a group of TIRA members.

# Lore (Vulcean Continuity)

The Vulcean Continuity takes place in the year 3000 (Terranian years) and the year 2445 (Earth years). Earth’s technology has improved significantly, especially in exoplanet science, but it still struggles to detect the planets of the Phebe System, except for one...

The Phebe System (which is home to the main civilisation of this story) is located about 21.9 light-years from the Sun in the constellation of Serpens Caput. It includes two stars: a G7-class yellow dwarf called Phebe and a K6-class orange dwarf named Amaros. Both stars orbit their shared centre of mass every 10.86 Earth days and are very close together. All of their planets, asteroids, and comets orbit both stars at the same time in a P-type orbital setup.

From Earth, Phebe and Amaros are listed as a detached eclipsing binary that shows ellipsoidal variation, a system that does not fit neatly into standard variable star classifications. Both stars stay well within their Roche lobes, so no mass transfer occurs between them. This means the system’s complex light behaviour is due to geometry and tidal interactions rather than any unusual evolutionary process. The mix of variability sources results in one of the most intricate light curves of any nearby star system, making it nearly impossible for human astronomers to differentiate a real planetary transit signal from the system’s own noise. Radial velocity searches have had similar results, as the two stars create large, unevenly timed Doppler shifts that mask any planetary signature. Stellar activity caused by tidally induced starspots adds more confusion. There are some tentative and hotly debated claims of a possible Jupiter-mass planet at several AU from the binary centre, based on residual radial velocity trends, but no detection has passed peer review. As of 2445, the Phebe System remains the most stubbornly uncharacterized nearby star system in human astronomical records, frustrating the exoplanet science community and keeping its secrets hidden.

The intelligent species living on Terrania, the Phebe System’s habitable world located within the binary’s habitable zone where liquid water can exist on the surface, call themselves Terranians. They know about humanity's existence and have been quietly observing the Solar System for some time. The fact that human astronomers have not yet unravelled the photometric complexity of their home system is what keeps Terranians calm, without worrying about a potential human invasion of their planet for resources and colonisation.

Terranian scientists are also researching Auron, another planet in the Phebe System, from dedicated orbiting stations. Auron is a mini-Neptune (a type of world that’s between a rocky planet like Earth and ice giants like Neptune), and it has attracted significant scientific interest after powerful space telescopes orbiting Terrania detected dimethyl sulfide and dimethyl disulfide in its atmosphere. On Terrania, these molecules mostly come from biological processes, making their possible presence in Auron’s atmosphere one of the most exciting and debated discoveries in modern Terranian science. Whether these signals indicate real biosignatures or unusual abiotic chemistry is still an open question that researchers are actively exploring.
