Venus | ♀

Overview

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in the Solar System. Its thick, toxic atmosphere traps heat through a runaway greenhouse effect. Venus is often called Earth's twin because of its similar size, but conditions on its surface are nothing like Earth.

Orbit & Distance

Venus orbits the Sun at an average distance of 108 million km, completing one orbit every 225 Earth days. It is the second planet from the Sun and the closest planet to Earth. At its nearest approach, Venus comes within about 40 million km of Earth, closer than any other planet.

Surface

Venus has a rocky surface shaped by volcanic activity. Scientists have identified over 1600 major volcanoes, along with vast plains of solidified lava. The surface is hidden from view by thick clouds, so most mapping has come from radar imaging by orbiting spacecraft.

Atmosphere & Temperature

Venus has a dense atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide, with clouds of sulfuric acid. This atmosphere traps heat so effectively that surface temperatures reach around 465 °C, hot enough to melt lead. Atmospheric pressure at the surface is about 92 times that of Earth, similar to the pressure found 900 meters underwater.

Rotation & Day Length

Venus rotates very slowly, and it spins in the opposite direction to most planets. One rotation takes 243 Earth days, longer than its 225-day orbit. This means a day on Venus is longer than its year.

Moons

Venus has no moons. Along with Mercury, it is one of only two planets in the Solar System without a natural satellite.

Exploration

Several spacecraft have studied Venus. The Soviet Venera missions were the first to land on its surface in the 1970s and 1980s. NASA's Magellan mission mapped the surface using radar in the early 1990s.
